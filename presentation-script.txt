WHAT CHANGED SINCE THE LAST EXPORT
Each beat now has a voiceover. It does not repeat the sentence on the screen. Cinema clocks are speech time, about 135 words a minute, plus a few seconds for the room to read the line.
The nature walk is cosmos, water, and the food web. Atmosphere, mycelium, and photosynthesis are out.
The body is back in full: energy, structure, blood, organs, lymph, nerves, then the brain, then the cortex.
Language, people, markets, satellites, and the internet are back, after the senses and before the shot. The living-loop captions stay out. The shot already runs that loop.
The Knicks inbound and the tip are back: the hold inside one skull, the branch, the possession, the make, then the celebration. The climb from that celebration up to Gage stays out.
The route chapter is no longer the zoomed failure tour. It is one aircraft, no one in the seat, then the wide fabric.
The agent does not replay the closed hub. People already kept that promise.
Observe, understand, decide, act, and verify are not their own chapter. The shot already ran the loop.
There is no booth and no questions. The show ends on three held frames, then the last line.

HOW TO SEND THIS BACK
Keep every line that starts with BEAT exactly as written, including the id.
Under each beat, edit only these three blocks:

ON SCREEN
The sentence on the frame. Delete the sentence if that frame should have no type.

BEAT
What the picture is doing. This is staging, not words you say.

NARRATION
What you say while that frame is up.

NARRATION is the voiceover. It should not repeat the sentence on the screen. The room reads that one. Cinema clocks are how long the voiceover takes at about 135 words a minute, plus a few seconds so the frame can be read, and never shorter than twelve seconds. The cold open stays silent. Conductor mode still ignores the clocks.
